`npm test` now passes: 24 tests, 0 failures. I made two fixes in the engine and changed one test.

- **Powers:** `^` now associates to the right, so `2 ^ 3 ^ 2` is 512. In `src/parser.ts` I changed its binding powers to `[41, 40]`.
- **Negatives:** unary minus now binds looser than `^` but tighter than `*`, so `-2 ^ 2` is -4 and `(-2) ^ 2` is 4. I changed `PREFIX_MINUS` from 50 to 30. `2 ^ -1` still gives 0.5.
- **Rounding:** `round` now rounds halves away from zero, so `round(-2.5)` is -3 and `round(-1.25, 1)` is -1.3. In `src/functions.ts` it rounds the absolute value and puts the sign back. Before, `Math.round` pushed negative halves toward zero.

The test `round half` asserted `round(2.5) === 2`. That contradicts the documented behaviour in `src/functions.ts` (half away from zero, "round(2.5) = 3") and the neighbouring negative-half test. I treated the test as the mistake and changed it to expect 3. If the pricing team's spreadsheet really gives 2 for 2.5 (banker's rounding), tell me, because the `round` fix would then need to change too.

I haven't addressed floating-point cases like `round(1.005, 2)`, where a spreadsheet may show 1.01 but this gives 1. No test covers that.
